# AITA NFL Full-Slate Forecast Board Architecture

## Objective

Generate a complete calibration-grade forecast record for every player-stat series produced by the football simulator on every slate, while preserving the separation between probability forecasting and price-sensitive betting decisions.

## Three Board Layers

### 1. Full Forecast Boards — market blind

Receiving, rushing and passing boards are generated directly from the frozen Monte Carlo distributions before sportsbook information is allowed to alter the football forecast.

Receiving contains:
- receptions
- receiving yards

Rushing contains:
- rushing attempts
- rushing yards

Passing contains:
- passing attempts
- completions
- passing yards

Every modeled row includes:
- event/player/team/opponent identity
- mean, SD, median, P10/P25/P75/P90
- model-native calibration reference line
- raw P(OVER), P(UNDER), P(push)
- most-probable side
- raw and initially calibrated pick probability
- model fair odds
- directional-strength label
- role certainty / model agreement / data freshness
- stable forecast ID and calibration key

The model-native reference line is a half-point nearest the model mean and is explicitly labeled as `MODEL_NATIVE`. It is not presented as a sportsbook line and must never be used to calculate EV.

### 2. Market-Lined Pick Boards — probability first

For each player/market that has an actual sportsbook line, the pipeline determines a consensus posted line and calculates BOTH OVER and UNDER probabilities directly from the frozen draws. The board records whichever side is more probable, even if that side does not clear an EV threshold.

This layer exists to measure forecast-side accuracy and probability calibration against real market propositions without confusing forecast quality with bet value.

### 3. EV Play Board — price sensitive

The existing EV board remains the only board that labels a proposition actionable at a reference price. It uses actual price, no-vig market probability, calibration/blending, uncertainty haircut, conservative EV, BUY-TO and hard-stop logic.

## Calibration Doctrine

A player can be:
- correctly forecast directionally but a bad bet at the available price;
- incorrectly forecast directionally despite having appeared +EV;
- accurately forecast in mean/quantiles even if no sportsbook line existed.

For that reason, forecast boards and EV boards must be audited separately.

## Snapshot Doctrine

Every `simulate` run creates an immutable Layer-1 forecast snapshot directory. This permits later comparison of early-week, mid-week and game-day football forecasts without overwriting history.

Python does not grade the forecast. The Trading Agent attaches actual results and closing-market observations in Dataset B. Later calibration research may join Dataset A and Dataset B by forecast/candidate identifiers.

## Coverage Rule

The forecast boards contain every player-stat series actually generated by the simulator. They do not fabricate forecasts for players outside the current simulated role universe. Backup quarterbacks or deep roster players with no modeled role are therefore not assigned fake prop predictions merely to fill a table.
